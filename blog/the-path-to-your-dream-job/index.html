Discover Your Dream Job

A model to understand where jobs fall in your path to find your dream career

Originally published on Rationale for Freaks, June 1, 2013.

When I got out of college in 2012, everybody was looking for their dream job. A year after, I can look back and say that not everybody got their dream job (even though most of my best friends got awesome positions at great companies). I realized how college students' expectations might be misaligned with the realities of the job market at any given time, especially if the market is coming back from an economic recession. With that in mind, I wanted to share with you a model that really helped me understand the role of my first job within my overall career. The first time I got exposed to this model was back in 2011 when I met with Marcelo Montero over coffee to talk about career (a mentorship activity), so here it is:

First of all, no one can really act outside of his or her personal values for an extended period of time; therefore, when we look at one's career we have to look within the "frames" (boundaries) of our personal values. For example, if honesty is one of your personal values, you would not work on a profession in which your honesty is compromised every day.

There are three main zones in which a position or a job could potentially fall:

  1. The Capabilities zone is the area in which you feel you have the ability and skill to do the job. You know that your position falls in this zone when you feel "Life is good." You are comfortable as what you do because it is aligned with what you are good at. An example of this could be someone who is good with numbers, and their job requires daily use of math.
  2. In Zone 2 we have Desired Experiences; these are experiences in life that a person would like to have. A great example are those people who live abroad because they thought at some point in their career that he/she would like to be working as an expat. Usually, when offered these kinds of desired experiences, they generate excitement and thoughts of "I wanna give it a try!"
  3. Passion is Zone 3. Jobs that fall in this zone energize the person and overwhelm them with excitement and enthusiasm. When you love what you do, you get raw energy out of it (an end in itself). In a past blog, I argued that it makes economical sense to spend time finding your passion. Spending your time working in a career that you are passionate about will be a smart rational and emotionally-positive decision. Good examples are soccer players who choose their career solely based on passion for the game.

In addition, a specific job or a position within a company could fall in any of these areas as well as in between them. Here are the mixed zones:

  1. Zone 4 is "Bring it On," the intersection between Capabilities and Desired Experiences. In this case, a person will be trying and learning new things, as well as using strong competencies and building them through new experiences. A recent CPA could be a good example, while taking a new position in accounting. That position is what he/she would like to do (desired experience) and he/she studied to acquire the necessary skills for it.
  2. "I Love What I Do" is Zone 5, in which Capabilities and Passion meet. In this area, you will be able to live and work on the things you love to do. You will most likely excel because work plays to your abilities; therefore, you will feel good. Good teachers are great examples. Good teachers are good at teaching and they draw their energy from doing what they do most, teaching.
  3. If your position/job falls between your Desired Experiences and Passion, you will be in "Pure Adrenaline" (Zone 6). This is a new and fun journey in which you leave your comfort zone to learn new things. It may be an art like music, drama, or painting. You may love the art (it's your passion), and you would like to pursue a career as an artist, but you don't necessarily have the capabilities on doing it. Anyhow, you take the challenge and jump on this new career.
  4. Lastly, there is "The Quoin" (Zone 7), the zone in which Passion intersects Capabilities and Desired Experiences. This is the sweet spot where it all makes perfect sense. You will be competent, passionate and learning new things in this position/job, in harmony with yourself (feeling a sense of accomplishment or purpose).

Marcelo named this model: "A Practical Way to Find Purpose." However, I didn't necessarily embrace this model to find purpose itself, but to understand how jobs fit within my career. In this regard, I was able to find a job/position compatible with my long-term career in mind, as soon as I was able to figure out where in this model it would fall.

Every time a Junior/Senior student or recent college grad tells me about his or her dream job, I first listen carefully, and then challenge them to think about long-term career and how future positions will fall under this model. In this way, if he/she is not able to find his/her dream job right out of college, at least they now have a tool to understand and a plan on how to get there.
