Six lenses on the US economy

Most economic dashboards hand you a wall of numbers and leave the interpreting to you. This one is built the other way round: 6 distinct analytical frameworks, each with its own view of what actually drives the economy, each shown with the 65 indicators its own logic says to watch.

They disagree with each other – sharply, and in ways that are measurable. Rather than average them into something bland, the first panel puts live data next to the points where they genuinely split, so you can see which reading the evidence is currently rewarding.

Everything here is drawn from public Federal Reserve data (FRED) – 147,094 observations across 57 series, going back to 1990.

Bank Credit Creation

Banks create money out of nothing when they lend, so bank credit growth – not the central bank – drives the economy. What matters is the purpose: credit for production grows GDP without inflation, credit for assets inflates bubbles, credit for consumption inflates prices.

The Quad Model

Growth and inflation on a rate-of-change basis define four regimes, and each regime has a repeatable asset-allocation playbook. Levels are opinion; rates of change are math.
