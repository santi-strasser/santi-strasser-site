Connectworking 1.0: Redefining Networking Through Authentic Connection

Redefining networking through authentic connection

Originally published on LinkedIn, May 18, 2015.

During the last month, I had the chance to share some of my knowledge on networking to more than 100 people. During these presentations, I'd ask how many people in fact do practice networking; the answer always came to somewhere between 5-10% of the audience. I've heard way too many people say "oh, yeah.. networking is important, I should do that." So, I've wondered why people don't fully engage in networking, if they seem to understand its value.

Maybe a misconception of networking underlies this passive behavior. That's why I wanted to re-define networking as Connectworking. In short, I think that if you get to know yourself and are clear on what you want, it just takes learning a good framework to be able to connectwork effectively. Otherwise, the only respectable excuse for not to connect with others would be: "I just don't want to do it."

Setting the record straight: asking for what you need or want is your responsibility

In the Brazilian culture, in which I find myself immerse these days, it's hard to ask for a favor. For the most part, I've observed people refraining themselves from asking a favor due to a perceived inability of the other person to say "NO." What do I mean by that? Brazilians may have a tendency to unconsciously think: "I rather not ask for what I need, in case the other person will have to say 'yes' and do something he/she doesn't really want to."

This doesn't seem to be very healthy, so to give this culture a "checkmate," I firmly state: The inability of others to say "NO" is out of your control. In fact, you have the privilege and responsibility to ask for what you want/need (nobody else can really do this for you!). The "NO" as an answer is 100% guaranteed until the moment you choose to ask.

The importance of networking: Opportunities = Weak ties x Ability to connect

According to the U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 70% of all jobs are found through networking (Yale & Cornell). This percentage is even higher for executives. So, you could agree with me that to truly tap on the job market, connecting is imperative.

By connecting, we can exponentially add more weak ties to our network, learn new information that could fuel future conversations, and gain 'personal ambassadors' (people that when opportunities arise, they will think of you).

Everyone is connected and weak ties matter

Six degrees of separation is the theory that states that everyone is six or fewer steps away from any other person in the world. Microsoft (in 2009), Twitter (in 2010), and Facebook (in 2011) have run experiments concluding that their data supports the six degrees of separation theory.

In addition, weak ties usually connect us to other networks of individuals. Weak ties are the people we have met, or are connected to somehow, but do not currently know well. "Weak ties are like bridges you cannot see all the way across, so there is no telling where they might lead" (Meg Jay). If opportunities arise outside of your tight network, then weak ties are more likely to connect you to those opportunities.

Know who you are and what you want, have a framework, and open yourself to others

Your ability to connect may well be indicated by your understanding of yourself (your Personal Brand: who are you?), the clarity of your life goals (being intentional on what really matters), your confidence in networking (recommended framework: the 20-minute networking meeting), and your ability to open yourself to others.

One of the deepest questions I got asked during a Connectworking talk was about the role of empathy in connecting. It is true that there might be at least 7 ways to fascinate others (Sally Hogshead), and yes, that helps us connect with others. However, the key is to open ourselves and show our vulnerability. When we authentically open up, others can relate to the humanity that we all share (Brené Brown). Authenticity and openness are key to deeply connecting with someone.

So, get started... get to know yourself and what you really want. Use a good framework to authentically connect with others. And remember, it's also ok if you are not ready for it and decide to say: "I just don't want to do it for now." It just takes courage, serenity, and wisdom – things that we acquire in part throughout this voyage that some call life. Happy Connectworking!
